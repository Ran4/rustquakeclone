# 18. Pressure & Flood — The Dam Actually Breaks

> Blow the spillway gate on the level-8 dam and the reservoir lowers second-by-second while the gorge below fills — a hazard volume that *moves its kill-line under your feet* in real time.

**The idea** — Today a hazard is a static box: a `LavaVolume` AABB that hurts anything inside it and a flat `kill_y` plane below the world. This feature makes a hazard volume's water-line *live*. The level-8 dam holds a high reservoir on one face and a fatal drop on the other. Breach the flood-gate (a Death Knight guards it before the exit) and two coupled volumes animate: the reservoir's top surface **drains** downward over ~20 seconds while the discharge gorge **floods** upward to meet it. The deadly region is no longer where it was when the fight started.

**Why it's fresh** — Most shooters fake water with a decorative plane and a scripted cutscene. Here the water *is* the simulation: the same swept-AABB volume that decides whether you take damage is the thing whose height is changing, so the lethal geometry and the visual surface are one object. The arena physically re-shapes itself mid-combat — drowning becomes a moving target, and a ledge that was safe at second 5 is underwater at second 15.

**How it plays** — Breaching starts a clock. The reservoir side becomes *crossable* as it drains (new footing appears), while the gorge side you fled across at the start steadily becomes a death-pool rising toward you. You're forced into a vertical race: grab the last items low on the reservoir floor before it's safe-but-empty, or sprint the flooding gorge catwalks before the rising water-line claims them. Knockback into a half-flooded pit is now lethal where a moment ago it was a bruise. Take the truck across before the deck-height fords over.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — Extends `level.rs`'s `LavaVolumes` resource (give a volume an animated top-Y and a fill/drain rate) and `gamestate.rs::lava_damage` (already does the per-frame `Aabb::overlaps` test — it just reads a moving height now). The rising surface borrows the **vehicle moving-collider trick**: optionally rewrite a `WorldColliders.solids` slot per frame so deep water *floats* you, exactly as `vehicle.rs` and the sliding doors rewrite their footprints. Triggered through the existing `door`/objective wiring in `gamestate.rs`; a draining-water loop slots into `audio_gen.rs`; the surface mesh reuses the emissive-pulse animator already in `animate_lava`.

**Build sketch** — Add a `rate`, `target_y`, and current `top_y` to hazard volumes; a system lerps `top_y` toward `target_y` once the gate-breach flag fires, updating both the damage test and a single quad-mesh's transform. The honest hard part is *interaction order*: the player's ground probe and the swept solver run against last frame's collider list, so a fast-rising surface must update before the move-and-slide pass or you'll clip a frame through it — and rider-carry on a rising "floor" needs the same one-frame-ahead ordering the truck already fights.

**Effort** — **M**. Main risk: frame-ordering between the animated water height, the swept-AABB ground probe, and rider-carry — get it wrong and the player jitters or tunnels through the surface.
