# 41. Nail Pin — Stake Them to the Wall

> A nail through the wing pins the Scrag to the ceiling. Now it just hangs there, twitching, while you reload.

**The idea** — A nail that strikes a monster while its body is sandwiched against a surface drives *through* it and stakes it there: a Scrag pinned to the ceiling, a Knight nailed flat to a pillar, an Ogre tacked to a wall. The pin roots the monster in place, killing its movement while it thrashes to tear free; the struggle has a fixed budget, and a second or third nail extends the root so a panicked clump can be sewn down for good. A pinned monster is still alive and still a body in the world — you can shove it into lava and hold it there to cook, and a pinned corpse stays as grisly wall décor instead of toppling. A charged nail pins harder, even catching a monster that is merely *near* a surface and slamming it the rest of the way.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake taught us to gib, not to *capture* — staking a living enemy to architecture turns the level itself into a restraint. The thinnest, fastest, most disposable projectile in the game becomes a tool of control, and the room's geometry suddenly matters as much as the monster's health bar. Pinned-into-hazard kills make positioning a weapon, not a footnote.

**How it plays** — You stop spraying nails into the open and start herding fights toward walls, columns and ceilings, then tacking the worst threat down while you deal with the rest. A nailed Knight is harmless for a few seconds — long enough to reload, or to nudge it off a ledge — and a Scrag stapled overhead is a free, drifting target. The new read is spatial: is this monster against something *right now*, and is there lava one shove away?

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — A nail body from `projectiles.rs` already carries a velocity and resolves its impact with `raycast_world` in `physics.rs`, which hands back both the hit point and the wall normal — exactly what we need to test whether the monster's AABB is sandwiched between the nail and a surface along that normal. On a confirmed pin, `combat.rs` knockback slams the monster flat to the surface and `enemies.rs` gains a `Pinned` stun state that overrides `Chase`/`Attack` and zeroes its movement for a duration, with extra nails refreshing the timer. The `monster_model.rs` animator keeps the skeletal rig twitching (a leaned-against-the-wall pain flinch) so a pin reads as *struggling*, not frozen. If the pin sits inside a `gamestate.rs` hazard `Aabb`, the existing 0.3s hazard DoT cooks it for free, and a pinned death leaves the rig stuck to the wall instead of running the death topple.

**Build sketch** — On nail-monster contact, raycast a short probe past the monster along the nail's travel; if it hits world within a body-width, set `Pinned { until, normal }` and snap the rig to the surface. The honest hard part is detecting "trapped against a wall" *robustly* — corners, thin brushes and the moving truck collider all confound a single probe — and guaranteeing the rooted state always releases so the AI never deadlocks if its anchor brush moves or vanishes.

**Effort** — **M.** Main risk: reliably detecting a monster sandwiched against a surface and ensuring the `Pinned` state always frees the AI rather than stranding it.
