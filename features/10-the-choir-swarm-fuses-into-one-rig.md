# 10. The Choir — A Swarm That Fuses Into One Rig

> A cloud of skittering motes converges, grafts its bones onto a shared root to become a single lumbering brute, and when you finally drop it the rig shatters back into motes that scatter and re-fuse somewhere darker.

**The idea** — The Choir is one monster that has two bodies. In its *dispersed* form it's a loose flock of small mote-creatures — each a stub rig (a head-jaw blob, a couple of clawed limbs), individually weak, swirling and pestering you. When enough survive within a radius they **converge on a shared root** and graft their skeletons onto it, assembling a tall, slow Ogre-class brute whose silhouette is visibly stitched from a dozen smaller bodies. Kill the fused brute and it doesn't just topple — it **fissions**: surviving motes detach as live entities, scatter to cover, and begin re-forming the brute elsewhere on the map.

**Why it's fresh** — Every other monster is a fixed rig. The Choir is the first whose *entity hierarchy mutates at runtime*: bones are re-parented onto a new root and back. It turns QUAKECLONE's "the skeleton IS the hierarchy" conceit into a mechanic — the swarm and the boss are literally the same parts, re-arranged. No swap to a prefab; the brute is the motes.

**How it plays** — You get a real decision the moment you hear the converge cue: **thin the swarm before it fuses** (chase scattering motes with the nailgun / lightning gun) or **let it assemble and burst the brute** (rockets, super shotgun) knowing each kill seeds the next swarm. Splash is your friend — a grenade into a converging cluster kills motes *and* denies the fusion. A whip fling or a truck ram into mid-converge scatters them. It rewards crowd-reading over single-target aim.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It leans hardest on `monster_model.rs`: the `BoneSpec`/`ChildOf` rig builder and the `Bone`/`AnimRole` per-frame animator already parent bones to a root, so fusion is *re-parenting* existing children onto a new root and re-tagging their owner. `enemies.rs` gets a fourth AI phase (Disperse) bridging Idle→Chase→Attack and a per-mote flock vel; `combat.rs`'s `Dying` topple is replaced by a fission spawn; `physics.rs` swept-AABB moves both forms; `effects.rs` sells the converge/burst with particles and screen shake; `audio_gen.rs` synthesises a chittering converge-rising "choir" cue.

**Build sketch** — Spawn motes as normal `Enemy` entities with a tiny rig and a flock velocity. A coordinator system watches mote density; once N cluster, spawn a root, **re-parent each mote's bones** onto it at assigned sockets (so the brute is genuinely built from them), freeze the motes' AI, and run the brute as one `Enemy` with summed health. On death, reverse it: detach bones back into mote entities, kick them outward, re-enable Disperse AI. The hard part is the re-parent/skin bookkeeping — bone `base_pos`/`base_rot` and per-instance `MonsterMats` are computed for the *original* root, so fusion must rebase each transform into the new root's space and stay glitch-free across two fuse/fission round-trips.

**Effort** — **L**. Main risk: runtime re-parenting of skinned bone hierarchies is fiddly (transform rebasing, material/owner re-tagging, despawn ordering) and a half-fused rig looks broken fast — needs a `QC_GALLERY`-style harness shot mid-fusion to tune.
