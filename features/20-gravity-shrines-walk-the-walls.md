# 20. Gravity Shrines — Walk the Walls to the Key

> Step into a shrine's field and "down" rotates onto the nearest wall — so you walk, jump and fight your way up an Escher box to a key no floor can reach.

**The idea** — A *gravity shrine* is a brush you author like any hazard: a glowing rune-plinth that radiates a field volume. Inside it, the player's local gravity axis snaps from world-down to a shrine-declared axis (a wall, the ceiling, an inward-facing face). Your footing, your step-up, your air-strafe and your jump all fire along that new axis. A vault built as a sealed cube becomes a six-walled track: the Silver Key sits on a "ceiling" ledge you can only reach by walking up a wall and crossing what was the roof. Monsters spawned inside the field share its gravity, so a Knight charges you *along the wall you're standing on*.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake never rotated gravity; Prey and *Manifold Garden* did it as set-piece puzzling, but never welded to bunny-hop-grade air-strafe movement and a swept-AABB solver. Here the wall-walk *is* the existing movement model with a re-pointed basis — same accel cap, same step-up, same wall-jump — so skill transfers and the room itself becomes the weapon.

**How it plays** — You hit the field and the camera rolls; the horizon tilts as the wall becomes the floor. Now every traversal decision doubles: do I rocket-jump off this face toward the key ledge, or walk the seam where two gravity zones meet and risk falling "up" into a lava ceiling? Wall-jumping between adjacent shrine faces becomes a real route. Combat re-frames too — a Scrag drifting near the ceiling is suddenly at your feet, and ramming logic still pulps whatever you charge along the wall.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — The core lives in `physics.rs`: the swept-AABB move-and-slide already resolves against an arbitrary axis if you feed it a rotated gravity basis instead of hard-coded world-down, so the step-up, ground probe and wall-jump come along. `player.rs` rolls the view to match; `level.rs`'s Build API gains a `gravity_shrine(volume, axis)` primitive beside `hazard`; `gamestate.rs` gates the key behind the field; `monster_model.rs` rigs and `enemies.rs` AI inherit the player's per-axis treatment for shared-gravity monsters. A field-enter whoosh and a low hum loop come from `audio_gen.rs`.

**Build sketch** — Store gravity as a per-entity unit vector, default world-down. A field-query system rotates it on enter/exit (lerped over a few frames to avoid nausea). The honest hard part is *seams*: when two fields overlap or a body straddles a field edge, the solver must hand off basis cleanly without the swept resolver oscillating or flinging the body — needs hysteresis and a dominant-field tiebreak. View-roll comfort and re-basing the air-strafe cap are the secondary fiddle.

**Effort** — **M**, leaning L if monster-shared gravity ships. Main risk: instability at field boundaries (jitter/eject) in the swept solver — prototype the basis handoff before authoring a level.
