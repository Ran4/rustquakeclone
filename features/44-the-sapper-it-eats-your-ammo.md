# 44. The Sapper — It Eats Your Ammo

> It doesn't want you dead. It wants your rockets — and it's already running off with them.

**The idea** — The Sapper is a skittering, spindly thief that doesn't fight you — it *robs* you. It darts in low and erratic, latches onto your back for a beat, and siphons off a chunk of your ammo (or briefly jams the weapon in your hands so the trigger does nothing), then breaks contact and *bolts for a corner* to stash the haul. The longer one survives, the lighter your pockets: a pack of three can strip-mine a full rocket count in seconds. Kill the Sapper before it offloads and the stolen rounds spill back out as a recoverable pickup; let it reach its stash and the loot is gone until you hunt down the hoard.

**Why it's fresh** — Every monster in an FPS is a damage-delivery system; the Sapper is the first one that *takes* instead of gives, and the first that flees the player on purpose. It inverts the core loop — suddenly the priority target is the one *not* shooting at you — and turns a firefight into a frantic protect-your-resources scramble while the Grunts keep peppering you. Quake never made you guard your own inventory mid-fight.

**How it plays** — A Sapper changes your whole target priority: ignore it and you'll reach for the Rocket Launcher to find it dry. You learn to keep a wall at your back, to save a fast hitscan weapon for the snatch-and-run, and to make the brutal call between finishing the Knight in your face and chasing the gremlin sprinting off with your cells. Drop it in time and you reclaim the spill; miss, and you're rationing for the rest of the room.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It's a new `enemies.rs` archetype with an inverted plan: the usual Idle -> Chase -> Attack machine becomes Approach -> Steal -> *Flee*, where the flee state paths *away* from the player toward a stash point — the opposite of every other monster's line-of-sight pursuit, but the same perception gate and pathing primitives. The steal "attack" mutates the `weapons.rs`/`common.rs` inventory directly (deduct ammo, or set a brief weapon-jam timer the firing code already could honour) instead of emitting a `DamageEvent`. `hud.rs` fires its damage flash plus a notification to announce the theft so it never feels invisible, and on death `combat.rs` drops the carried haul as a `pickups.rs` ammo box — reusing the gib/death path it already runs. The rig is just another `monster_model.rs` skeleton, a lean four-legged scuttler with a grasping arm.

**Build sketch** — Add a `Sapper` kind with a `stolen: Ammo` accumulator and a `flee_target` pulled from the nearest wall corner; in the AI, when latched within reach, transfer a clamped chunk of the player's ammo, play a snatch `audio_gen.rs` sting, then switch to the flee state that steers down the navmesh away from `Player`. On death, spend `stolen` into a dropped pickup; on reaching its stash, zero it for good. The honest hard part is *fairness* — telegraphing the latch, a generous steal cooldown so a swarm can't instantly zero you, and away-from-player flee pathing that doesn't wedge the thing into a corner or a moving truck.

**Effort** — **M.** Main risk: making resource theft feel fair (clear telegraph + cooldown) and getting the away-from-player flee pathing to behave around brushes.
