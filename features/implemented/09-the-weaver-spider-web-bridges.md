# 09. The Weaver — Spider That Spins Walkable Web Bridges

> A wall-crawling arachnid that strings load-bearing silk across the void — and you only get to use it as long as you don't shoot it down.

**The idea** — The Weaver is a new spider monster that doesn't just attack: it *builds*. When it perches on an anchor point it spins a taut silk strand to another anchor, and that strand becomes a thin, walkable surface — a real collider you can stand on, walk along and fight from. A Weaver mid-arena can rope two ledges together over a hazard pit; a nest of them can lattice an open chamber into a climbable web you have to read on the fly. Each strand is solid until it isn't: sever the silk (shoot the strand or kill the spider anchoring it) and the bridge vanishes, dropping anything riding it — you, a chasing Knight, the spider itself.

**Why it's fresh** — Most FPS enemies subtract from the arena (they shoot you, they block a door). The Weaver *adds* geometry that obeys the same physics you do, then makes that geometry destructible and contested. The terrain is the enemy and the enemy is the terrain. Nothing in the campaign currently lets a monster reshape where the player can stand.

**How it plays** — A strand is a tightrope: cross it to reach a key ledge the Weaver was guarding, and now you've used the enemy's own work against it. But the spiders shimmy along their own webs to flank you, and other monsters path across them too — so cutting a strand is a live tactical choice. Sever it under a charging Ogre and it plummets into the lava; sever it under yourself by accident and so do you. Wall-jump and rocket-jump become escape hatches when the floor you trusted evaporates.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It's the truck trick generalised. Each strand reserves a thin slot in `WorldColliders.solids` (physics.rs) and rewrites it from its endpoints every frame, exactly like vehicle.rs and the sliding doors — so the swept-AABB solver, monster pathing, hitscan and LoS treat it as solid for free. Severing reverts the slot to empty, the same disable a door's collider already does in gamestate.rs. The spider is a procedural skeletal rig (monster_model.rs: a hub body, eight faceted vertex-jittered legs, a wall-crawl/spin animation), with a strand-spin attack added to its Idle→Chase AI (enemies.rs). Authors place anchors and Weavers through the level.rs Build API. A wet spinneret-click and a snapping-cable crack go in audio_gen.rs.

**Build sketch** — The rig and AI are well-trodden ground. The honest hard part is the strand collider: an AABB is axis-aligned, but a silk bridge wants to run at any yaw and any pitch. A single thin box reads fine for a near-horizontal strand; a steeply angled one is approximated by a short stair of stacked AABB segments along the line, each its own reserved slot, all rewritten together. Rider-carry already exists (vehicle.rs), but riders here don't move with the strand — they just need clean fall-off the instant the slots clear, which the solver gives for free once the AABBs disappear. Budget the slot count: cap concurrent strands so the flat `solids` slice stays small.

**Effort** — **M**. Main risk: the diagonal-strand collider. If stacked-AABB approximation feels lumpy underfoot, fall back to horizontal-only strands between same-height anchors — still a strong mechanic, much cheaper.
