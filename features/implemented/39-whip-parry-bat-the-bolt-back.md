# 39. Whip Parry — Bat the Bolt Back

> The Scrag spits, the Enforcer fires — and you swat it out of the air, back down its own throat.

**The idea** — A well-timed Whip swing inside a tight window stops being a shove and becomes a *parry*. If an incoming hostile projectile — an Enforcer bolt, a gob of Scrag spit, a lobbed Ogre grenade — crosses the Whip's arc during the parry window, you don't take it; you bat it. A clean parry reflects the projectile's velocity forward off the plane of your swing and flips it hostile-to-friendly, so the deflected shot screams back along the reflection of its own path; a perfectly-timed return that lands on the shooter kills them with their own ammunition. A late or sloppy swing still *pops* the projectile harmlessly out of the air — you eat nothing, but you also get no free kill. The no-ammo Whip finally earns a defensive identity to go with its knockback.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake projectiles are things you *dodge*, never things you *interact with* — there's no melee answer to a bolt in the genre's DNA. A read-and-react deflect turns enemy fire into a resource, and the perfect-parry window rewards learning each monster's tell instead of just strafing forever. It makes the weakest, ammoless weapon the highest-skill one.

**How it plays** — Against a Scrag wall or a line of Enforcers you stop juking and start *timing*: hold the Whip, read the spit, swing on the beat, and turn three incoming bolts into three dead shooters without spending a shell. The decision is constant — parry now and risk a whiff, or just keep dodging — and a botched window means you wear the bolt you tried to swat. It's a stylish, ammo-free clutch tool that turns a corner full of fire into a turkey shoot in the other direction.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It bolts onto systems that already exist. `projectiles.rs` already owns enemy bolts, Scrag spit and grenades as bodies each carrying a velocity, so a parry is just a velocity rewrite plus an owner flip. The parry hitbox is the Whip's existing `melee_strike` raycast/arc in `weapons.rs`, fired with a short active window; reflecting the projectile's velocity off the swing plane is the very same maths `physics.rs` already does when `move_and_slide` bounces a body off the wall normal `raycast_world` hands back. `combat.rs` reassigns the deflected projectile's owner to the player so its impact runs the normal player-damage path against monsters (armor absorb, splash, knockback for a returned grenade), and a sharp metallic *ting* synthesised in `audio_gen.rs` plus a tiny `effects.rs` spark sell the connect.

**Build sketch** — Give the Whip a brief `parry_window` timer on swing; each frame it's open, sweep the Whip arc against every enemy-owned body in `projectiles.rs`, and on overlap reflect its velocity about the swing-plane normal, clear its hostile flag and set its owner to the player (a perfect-window hit gets a speed and homing-toward-tell bonus, a late hit just despawns it). The honest hard part is *fairness*: the window and arc must be generous enough to feel earned, not lucky, and tight enough not to be a passive bullet-shield — and ownership reassignment has to be airtight so a returned grenade never friendly-fires the player who sent it back.

**Effort** — **M.** Main risk: tuning the parry window and arc for fairness while cleanly reassigning projectile ownership without self-damage.
