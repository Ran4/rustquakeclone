# 38. Terminal Velocity — Fall Damage Becomes a Weapon

> Stop fearing the drop — a mid-air slam turns every metre you fell into a shockwave on the floor below.

**The idea** — Tap a slam input in mid-air and the player snaps straight down at a hard fixed acceleration, riding gravity past terminal speed; the instant the boots touch ground it fires a radial ground-pound shockwave at the landing point. The blast's damage, knockback and radius — plus the screen shake, dust ring and gibs — all scale off the *impact speed* the player carried in, so a hop fizzles but a long plummet detonates like a rocket. It is the truck-ram philosophy made vertical: fast equals lethal, and the metres you fell are the ammunition. Rocket-jump up over a Knight pack and come down through the middle of them, or take a forced fall off the level 8 dam crest and weaponise it instead of dying to it.

**Why it's fresh** — Fall damage in shooters is a punishment tax you route *around*; here it is a resource you bank and spend, inverting the genre's oldest "don't fall" reflex. Quake taught players to fear gravity and master the rocket-jump to cheat it — this hands gravity back as an offensive verb that pairs with the rocket-jump rather than just surviving it. No other weapon in the kit charges by altitude.

**How it plays** — Height becomes a loaded gun: every ledge, catwalk and rocket-jump arc is now a windup, and the decision is whether to spend the fall as a slam or save your footing. The slam commits you to a vertical line — you cannot strafe out of it — so you read the pack below, pick the moment, and trade air control for a guaranteed area detonation, then scramble before the survivors close in.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — `player.rs` already integrates vertical velocity on `Player.vel` and resolves ground contact each frame through `physics.rs` `move_and_slide`, so the slam is just a forced downward accel plus a landing edge-detect that reads the impact `vel.y`. The shockwave is `combat.rs`'s radius splash with falloff and knockback — the explosion minus the rocket — spawned at the contact point with its radius and damage curved off impact speed, reusing the same armour and gib paths. `effects.rs` supplies the screen shake, a fresh dust-ring particle burst and the gib spray, scaled by the same speed value, while `audio_gen.rs` synthesises a deep impact thud whose volume tracks the hit. Monsters caught in the ring take it exactly like a grenade blast, so the `enemies.rs` knockback and `combat.rs` death/gib code need no special case.

**Build sketch** — Add a `Slam` flag and a downward-accel branch in `player.rs` movement; on the grounded transition, if the flag is set and `vel.y` exceeds a threshold, map speed through a curve to call the existing splash routine and effects, then clear the flag and refund a little forward control. The honest hard part is tuning that speed-to-radius curve so it richly rewards committed altitude without becoming a spammable get-off-me panic button on every two-foot step.

**Effort** — **S.** Main risk: balancing the speed-to-blast curve so it rewards real commitment instead of becoming a spammable panic button.
