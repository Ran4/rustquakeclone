# 28. Noise Budget — Diegetic Sound Stealth

> Every sound the player makes already has a volume and a position — let the monsters *hear* it, so a sloppy bunny-hop landing wakes the room before anyone sees you.

**The idea** — Every `Sfx` you emit (`common.rs`: a `Sound`, a `pos`, a `volume`) becomes a *noise event* in the world, not just an audio cue. When the shotgun roars or your boots slam a `Land`, that event radiates from its position; any enemy whose ear is within the noise's effective radius — measured by actual ray-distance through the level, not a naive sphere — flips `Idle → Chase` with **zero line of sight**. Loud sounds (Explosion, SuperShotgun) carry across the map; a careful walk carries nowhere. Sound becomes a second perception channel sitting beside the eyes monsters already have.

**Why it's fresh** — Most shooters fake "they heard you" with a hidden alert meter. Here the trigger *is* the diegetic audio you can hear yourself: the loudness the player's own speakers play is literally the loudness the AI reacts to. One number drives both. There's no separate stealth stat to tune against the sound design — they're the same data.

**How it plays** — A new layer of intent over the same movement. Do you bunny-hop the gap and announce yourself with a thudding `Land`, or creep and stay a ghost? Do you open with the Whip — silent, no `Sfx::global` gunshot — to clear a sentry before the room knows you exist? A missed rocket that detonates on a far wall now pulls a pack you never saw. The truck on level 8 becomes a rolling alarm; flooring the engine summons everything ahead of you. Players learn the level's "noise map": which shots are safe where.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It rides existing rails. `Sfx` already carries `pos` and `volume`, so noise events are free to author. Propagation reuses `physics.rs` `line_of_sight`/`raycast_world` against `colliders.solids` for ray-distance falloff. The wake check slots straight into the `!en.awake` branch in `enemies.rs` — today gated on `dist < en.sight && los`; we add an OR for "heard it." `monster_model.rs` rigs already have a head/ear bone to sample. Per-`Sound` loudness lives next to the WAV table in `audio_gen.rs`/`common.rs`. The `Land`/`Jump` emitters in `player.rs` and the engine loop in `vehicle.rs` need no changes — they already shout.

**Build sketch** — Add a `loudness` weight per `Sound` and a small `NoiseEvent` buffer the `play_sfx`/`Sfx` path mirrors into. Each frame, sleeping monsters test pending events: skip if `volume * loudness` is below the ear-distance falloff, where distance is the swept ray length to the source (occluded paths attenuate, so a closed `door` muffles you). On a hit, wake and seed `Chase` toward the *sound's* position, not the player's — so they investigate stale noise and can be baited. The honest hard part is cost and feel: an N-monsters × M-events ray sweep every frame must be budgeted (cheap squared-distance pre-cull before any raycast), and the loudness table needs hand-tuning so stealth is readable, not a coin-flip.

**Effort** — **M.** Main risk: tuning the loudness/falloff curve so "I got heard" always feels fair and legible rather than random, plus keeping the per-frame ray sweeps off the level-8 frame budget.
