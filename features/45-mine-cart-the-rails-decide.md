# 45. Mine Cart — The Rails Decide, Until You Blow Them

> The truck goes where you steer. The cart goes where the rails say — until you shoot the rails.

**The idea** — A rideable ore cart bolted to an authored rail spline that snakes through the level 3 foundry and the level 4 tomb: it crests a grade, screams down the far side, and tears through the molten channel while you ride and fight from the deck, leaning back on **S** to drag the brakes and **W** to let it run. The track is fixed — you don't steer it — but you *can* break it. A pellet through a rail section, or a hit on a junction switch ahead, redirects the cart onto a branch or rips the spline out from under it entirely. The hook is the exact inverse of the free-driving truck: here the path is decided for you until you choose, mid-plunge, to unmake it.

**Why it's fresh** — Rail rides in shooters are scripted set-pieces you sit through; this one is a *destructible* track you author your own line through with the gun. Quake has never had a vehicle whose steering input is "shoot the world," and the tension of a fixed path you can only alter by destroying it — gambling that a derail saves you rather than dumps you in the lava — is a verb no FPS rollercoaster offers.

**How it plays** — You're a passenger with a trigger, reading switches and weak rail joints rushing toward you and deciding whether to hold the line or blow it. Brake into the molten-channel bend or carry speed and risk overshooting the gap; snipe a junction to take the high branch past an Ogre nest; or, cornered, derail on purpose and let the cart tumble into the pack as a wrecking ball. Every section is a fast read under fire.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — The cart is the moving-collider object `vehicle.rs` and the doors already pioneered: it reserves one `WorldColliders` slot and rewrites it each frame from its transform, so player collision, monster pathing, hitscan and line-of-sight treat it as solid for free, and the per-frame rider-carry step slides whoever's on the deck along its motion. Instead of a free velocity vector it's advanced along a polyline — a scalar arc-position plus speed integrated against grade — but it still calls `move_and_slide` from `physics.rs` so it shoves and bounces correctly. `level.rs`'s Build API gains a `rail(points, switches)` authoring call alongside `corridor_z`/`door`. You fire normally through `weapons.rs`; a shot that hits a rail or switch (resolved via `raycast_world`) flips a branch or detaches the cart, handing it to `vehicle.rs`'s free velocity + angular-velocity physics so it tumbles, rams and pulps monsters through `combat.rs`, reusing the truck's engine/screech/clang audio in `audio_gen.rs`.

**Build sketch** — A `RailCart` component holding the active spline, an arc-length position and a speed driven by grade plus brake input; a follow system samples the polyline each frame to set transform and rewrite the reserved collider slot, with junctions as branch nodes a shot toggles. Derail clears the spline binding and seeds a `Vehicle` velocity from the last tangent, dropping straight into the existing free-physics loop. The honest hard part is making a spline-followed body coexist with the static-world swept solver — it can't tunnel a wall mid-curve — and a clean rails-to-free-physics handoff that doesn't snap the rider off on the transition frame.

**Effort** — **M/L.** Main risk: spline-follow against the static-AABB swept solver plus a seamless rails-to-free-physics derail handoff that keeps the rider aboard.
