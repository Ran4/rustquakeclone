# 55. Carnage Stains — The Room Remembers Every Kill

> Walk back through a room you cleared and read the fight off the walls: blood here, a scorch ring there, drag-marks to the door.

**The idea** — The world keeps a ledger of violence. Where a monster dies, blood splatters the floor and the wall behind it; where a rocket detonates, a black scorch ring sears the brush face it kissed; a shotgun blast that misses chews a tight cluster of pellet pocks into the plaster; a shoved or rammed corpse leaves a wet smear trail back to where it came to rest. These are persistent decal quads that accumulate for the life of the level, so an arena you fought through tells the story of the fight when you backtrack across it. Unlike Damage Decay, which carves the *monster's* rig, this marks the *world* — the geometry stays whole, but it never looks pristine again.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake-era shooters wiped blood and scorch after a few seconds to save fill rate; here the carnage is permanent for the level, so a cleared room is a readable battlefield instead of a reset stage. Combined with the per-dimension palette, the stains become part of the look: rust-red gore on Frostspire's blue ice reads completely differently than green ichor in the Verdant Rot. The room becomes a record, not just a stage you passed.

**How it plays** — Backtracking — already a Quake staple while you hunt the Silver Key — gains texture: a wall thick with pellet pocks tells you that's where you panicked, a long drag-smear tells you a corpse went *that* way under a ram. You start using the marks as breadcrumbs in a maze, reading "I've been here, I cleared this" off the gore instead of memorising bland corridors. It rewards the truck's pulping rams with the ugliest, most satisfying smears in the game.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It hangs entirely off events that already fire at known points with known surfaces. `combat.rs` already spawns blood and gibs in `check_deaths` and explosions via radius splash, and `effects.rs` already emits particles and fireballs at impact points — both gain a "drop a decal here" call. The orientation comes free from `physics.rs`: `raycast_world` already hands hitscan and projectile impacts back the wall normal, so a decal quad is just a flat-shaded mesh laid flush to that normal a hair off the brush face. Decal colour and grime come from the active `ThemeSpec` in `level.rs` (the same source as `tint`/`accent`/`fog`), and corpse smears ride the `effects.rs` carry already used when a `vehicle.rs` deck slides riders. Everything lives and dies on the level timeline `gamestate.rs` already owns, despawned wholesale on the exit-slipgate transition.

**Build sketch** — A small `Decals` resource pools a fixed ring buffer of quad entities; spawning past the cap recycles the oldest, so a marathon fight is O(cap) not O(kills). Each decal is one thin double-sided quad mesh oriented to the stored normal, with a tinted gore/scorch/pock texture and a tiny per-instance random rotation so they don't tile. The honest hard part is projecting cleanly onto arbitrary angled brush faces (a flat quad clips through corners and floats on convex trim) and tuning the hard pool cap low enough that a drawn-out Death Knight brawl never tanks the frame rate on the level-8 dam.

**Effort** — **S.** Main risk: decal quads z-fighting or clipping on angled and corner brush faces, plus a tight enough pool cap to stay free.
