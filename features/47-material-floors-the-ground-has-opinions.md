# 47. Material Floors — The Ground Has Opinions

> Level 2 already has ice you slide on. Make every floor lie to your feet like that — promote the surface underfoot to a first-class movement input.

**The idea** — Every brush carries a *floor material*, and that material rewrites how movement behaves while you stand on it. Ice is near-frictionless so you carry speed and overshoot; mud and tar drag and bleed your acceleration so a sprint becomes a wade; conveyor grates and slick metal duct-tops add a constant push that slides you (and anything else) along a fixed direction; ringing steel plate just changes the footstep timbre. Each dimension theme owns its palette — frost halls lean icy, the bio-hive oozes sticky sludge, the foundry pushes you along moving belts — so the same WASD inputs produce a different feel per level. It generalises level 2's one-off ice patch into a rule the whole campaign speaks.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake floors are uniform: friction is a global constant and the only "material" trick the genre ships is the occasional ice room. Making surface friction, acceleration and push *per-brush* and *per-theme* turns the floor itself into a level-design verb, on the same footing as a door or a hazard. It re-frames traversal as a negotiation with the ground rather than a constant — the map can speed you up, glue you down, or shove you sideways without a single trigger entity.

**How it plays** — You read the floor before you commit: charge a frost corridor and you'll skate past the turn, so you brake early or ride the slide on purpose into a strafe. Tar makes a room a kill-box — you can't dodge, so you clear it before stepping in, and you can lure a Knight into the same glue. A conveyor belt over a lava channel is a moving tightrope: stand still and it carries you, fight it to hold position, or let it sling you across the gap.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — The whole point is that the machinery already exists. `player.rs` friction and ground/air acceleration are the heart of movement; each frame, sample the material of the brush under the `physics.rs` ground-probe contact and scale the friction/accel terms before they touch `Player.vel`, leaving the bunny-hop and air-strafe cap untouched when airborne. Brushes get a `Material` tag through the `level.rs` Build API, defaulted from the active theme palette alongside its textures and fog. Conveyor push reuses the `vehicle.rs` rider-carry delta — add the belt's per-frame translation to riders exactly the way the truck deck slides the player and monsters — and `enemies.rs` AI inherits the same slip and drag for free since it moves on the same probe. Footstep timbre keys off the material via an `audio_gen.rs` synth variant.

**Build sketch** — Add a `Material` enum (with friction/accel/push fields) stored per `WorldColliders.solids` entry, return the hit index from the ground probe, look it up, and feed scaled coefficients into the existing friction/accel maths; conveyors add their push vector through the carry step. The honest hard part is a clean per-contact lookup at seams (two brushes under one foot) and tuning the icy/sticky extremes so they never quietly break the air-strafe and bunny-hop feel players rely on.

**Effort** — **S.** Main risk: a clean per-contact material lookup at brush seams without wrecking the bunny-hop/air-strafe feel.
