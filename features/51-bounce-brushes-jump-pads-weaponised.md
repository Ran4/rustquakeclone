# 51. Bounce Brushes — Quake Jump Pads, Weaponised

> Quake had jump pads. This one launches you — and your grenades, your barrels, and the Knight you kicked onto it.

**The idea** — A bounce brush is the classic Quake jump pad, but its top face flings *anything* that touches it along an authored impulse vector, not just the player. Stand on it and you're rocketed across the room; bounce a grenade onto it and it banks up over a wall into the vault; punt a powder keg or a charging Knight onto one and it sails into the lava. Each pad carries an authored direction and power (vertical, or angled to throw you diagonally), and it composes with whatever horizontal momentum the thing already had — so a fast approach plus a pad arcs you much further than a standing hop. Chain a few across a chasm and the level becomes a string of launches you steer between with air-strafe.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake's jump pads only ever launched the player; here the trigger is universal, so the pad becomes a piece of combat geometry, not just traversal. Redirecting a *grenade* off a pad turns a dead-end into a trick shot, and booting a *monster* onto one weaponises the level itself — you fight with the architecture instead of around it. It also reframes knockback: any shove that lands an enemy on a pad becomes a free aerial kill.

**How it plays** — You read pads the way you read ramps now: as launch lines to bank shots and bodies off. The new decisions are spatial and timed — approach a pad fast or slow, lead a grenade so it lands on the face mid-bounce, whip a Knight *toward* the pad rather than away. Pads are symmetric danger, too: get knocked onto one yourself and you're flung off the ledge, so positioning around them becomes its own duel.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — The pad is just another brush authored through the `level.rs` Build API as `b.jumppad(dir, power)`, emitted with world-aligned UVs and pushed as a `WorldColliders` solid like any wall. Detection reuses the existing ground-contact result from `physics.rs`'s `move_and_slide` / ground probe: when the resolved contact is a pad-tagged collider, the launch system overwrites `Player.vel` in `player.rs` with the pad's vector (keeping retained horizontal speed, exactly the surf-style velocity write `player.rs` already does). The same per-frame overlap test fires the impulse on `projectiles.rs` bodies, `effects.rs` gibs, and `enemies.rs` monsters — all of which carry a velocity — and even on the `vehicle.rs` truck's velocity vector. A glowing emissive pad face uses the existing HDR/bloom path, and a springy boing is synthesised in `audio_gen.rs`.

**Build sketch** — A `JumpPad { launch: Vec3 }` component on the brush entity plus a `last_launch` debounce stamp; one system iterates moving bodies, AABB-overlaps them against pad faces, and on a fresh contact sets `body.vel = body.vel.reject_from(up) + launch` so vertical is replaced but horizontal carries. The honest hard part is **firing exactly once per contact** — a body resting on the pad must not re-launch every frame (double-launch / infinite trampoline), so each body needs a short cooldown or an off-pad reset before it can be flung again, and the impulse-vs-retained-momentum blend wants clamps so a truck-speed approach doesn't yeet the player out of the map.

**Effort** — **S.** Main risk: debouncing one launch per contact without it feeling sticky, and composing the impulse with retained momentum so fast approaches don't fling bodies out of bounds.
