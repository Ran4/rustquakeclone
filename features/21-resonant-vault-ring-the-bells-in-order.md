# 21. The Resonant Vault — Ring the Bells in Order

> A key-vault that opens only when you strike its faceted bell-pillars in the right order — and the room's ambient drone is quietly humming you the combination.

**The idea** — A locked Silver Key sits behind a sealed vault face flanked by four-to-six tall, faceted bell-pillars, each tuned to a distinct procedurally-synthesised tone. Strike a pillar — a hit from any weapon, or a Whip crack — and it rings, flashes its emissive trim, and registers as the next note in a sequence. Ring all of them in the right order and the vault grinds open. Ring a wrong one and the chord sours, the lit pillars dim, and you start over. The catch: a low ambient loop in the room is *playing the answer* — the same tones, in order, woven into the level's atmosphere. Listen, then play it back on the pillars.

**Why it's fresh** — Quake clones use sound as feedback; this makes the synth itself a *puzzle channel*. The procedural audio that's normally garnish (footsteps, gunfire) becomes the actual lock mechanism — a Simon-says encoded in timbre rather than text or color. Nothing in the campaign asks you to *listen* to win; this does, and it does it with no new asset pipeline — the tones are generated, not recorded.

**How it plays** — You enter, hear a slow recurring motif, and have to map each pitch to its pillar (the lit flash on each ring teaches the pairing). It's a deliberate breather between firefights that rewards attention — but it stays Quake, because monsters can be alerted into the room while you solve it, so you're ringing bells with a Knight closing in. Skilled players memorise the motif on the first loop and snap through it; rushers brute-force and eat the reset.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — The tones extend `audio_gen.rs` directly: its existing `sine_sweep`/`Buf` primitives and decay envelopes already synth clean tonal hits — a bell is a struck partial-stack with a long decay, one per pitch. Detection rides existing hitscan/projectile contact and the `physics.rs` raycast already used for the Whip and shots. Pillars are faceted vertex-jittered meshes in the established world-brush style with emissive trim for the bloom pass. The sequence state, reset, and "vault opens" hand off to the `Door` + `Mission` objective machine in `gamestate.rs`, and the whole arrangement is laid with the `level.rs` `Build` API (`room`, `door`, `item`, `light`) plus one new `bell` helper.

**Build sketch** — Add a small `BellPuzzle` resource: ordered pillar IDs, the target sequence, current progress. A system watches damage/contact messages against pillar entities, plays that pillar's `Sfx`, advances or resets the sequence, and on completion fires the existing key-door open. The ambient teaching loop is one more synthesised clip scheduled on a timer. The honest hard part is *legibility*: the combination must be unambiguously audible over combat and the procedural noise floor, so the teaching motif needs spacing, a distinct register from weapon SFX, and probably a visual echo (each pillar pulsing in turn during the loop) so the puzzle is fair to players who miss the audio.

**Effort** — **M**. Synthesis, hit-detection, and door hand-off are all reuse; the real risk is tuning the puzzle to read clearly amid gunfire without a UI crutch — get that wrong and it feels like guessing.
