# 50. Collapse — The Floor Is Leaving

> The dam is coming apart behind you one slab at a time. Do not look back. Do not stop.

**The idea** — A scripted set-piece that turns a stretch of level into a forward-only chase by *deleting the ground behind you*. As you advance along the level-8 dam crest (or a foundry catwalk), the brushes you just crossed detach in sequence and fall away — the floor leaves on a timer, or, meaner, the instant your footsteps trip each segment. A detached slab isn't deleted: its world-collider footprint is pulled and the freed chunk drops as a tumbling solid, so you watch the catwalk peel into the gorge a reach behind your heels. Outrun it on foot, or floor the truck across the span before the deck under it is gone.

**Why it's fresh** — Collapse set-pieces in shooters are almost always a cutscene or a non-interactive backdrop crumble. Here the leaving floor is the *same simulated geometry you stand on* — the brush that vanishes is a real collider, and falling through it kills you exactly like any drop. Quake never moved its own brushwork against you; making the arena subtract itself in real time, frame-fair, is a new pressure on the classic run-jump loop.

**How it plays** — There is exactly one direction and it is forward — hesitation is death, and the bunny-hop and air-strafe suddenly matter for survival, not just speed. New decisions appear under the clock: detour for the item ledge and risk the floor reaching you, or abandon it; jump the gap a collapsing pier just opened, or thread the truck through before its lane drops. A wall-jump off a still-standing pier can save you when the catwalk goes early; a rocket-jump buys a slab of distance.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — It's the inverse of the Cartographer/door trick: instead of writing a moving collider into `WorldColliders.solids`, a flagged-fragile brush has its slot *pulled* and the chunk handed to a lightweight `vehicle.rs`-style mover that integrates a downward velocity and tumble — falling rubble all the player's `physics.rs` `move_and_slide` and `raycast_world` already treat as gone the moment the slot clears. `gamestate.rs` triggers sequence the detachments along the route, either on a timer or on a player-position tripwire reusing its per-frame `Aabb` overlap test; `level.rs`'s Build API gains a `fragile` flag on the brushes it already emits as both mesh and collider. `effects.rs` sells the cave-in with dust particles, screen shake and view-bob jolt, while `audio_gen.rs` scrubs a looping rumble louder as more slabs go — and it composes straight with `vehicle.rs` (outrun the gap in the truck) and the dam-break flood.

**Build sketch** — Tag fragile brushes at build time with a `Collapse` component holding a delay; a sequencer system fires them in order (timer or footstep trigger), each transfering its `WorldColliders` slot into a falling-rubble mover that integrates gravity + spin and despawns past the `kill_y` plane. Reuse the truck's collider-slot lifecycle so freeing and re-using slots stays cheap. The honest hard part is *frame-fair footing* — the next foothold must exist the instant the player commits, so detachment must lead the player by a tuned margin and never strand them, and many simultaneous fallers must stay cheap enough not to spike the mover system or the collider list.

**Effort** — **L.** Main risk: sequencing detachment so there's always a frame-fair next foothold while a crowd of simultaneous fallers stresses the mover and collider-slot churn.
