# 52. Cathedral Verb — The Room Shapes the Sound

> A shotgun in a vent is a dry crack. The same shotgun on the dam crest is a rolling boom that takes a second to die.

**The idea** — The geometry of wherever you're standing decides how everything sounds. Every frame the engine fires a small fan of probe rays from the player's eye, measures how far each one travels before it hits a wall, and folds those distances into a single live "bigness" metric — how enclosed and how large the surrounding volume is. That metric drives a procedural reverb send applied to every gunshot, footstep, impact and monster growl: a cramped vent corridor sounds dead and dry, a vast hall blooms with a long tail, and the mix slides continuously between the two as you move. Tight rooms also tighten the early reflections into a fast slap; open spaces stretch them into a slow, spreading wash.

**Why it's fresh** — Most shooters bake reverb into hand-painted volumes an artist drew around each room. This computes it live from the actual brushwork the player is standing in, so a hall that opens up as you cross it, or a door sliding shut behind you, audibly closes the space in real time. For a Quake clone the whole point is hand-built brush geometry — letting that geometry *speak* turns the level itself into the instrument.

**How it plays** — You start to hear the map before you see it: a long bloom on your own shotgun tells you you've stepped into a cathedral-sized vault even in the fog. Firing in a cramped pocket stays dry and surgical, while the dam crest answers a rocket with a boom that rolls for a full second, and the shift as you cross a doorway becomes a navigation cue in its own right. It also raises the stakes on noise — a shot that booms is a shot the whole vast room heard.

**How it fits QUAKECLONE** — The room probe is a few cheap calls into `physics.rs` `raycast_world`, which already returns hit distance against the same `&[Aabb]` solids — including the per-frame moving colliders that the doors and the `vehicle.rs` truck rewrite, so a closing flood-gate or a truck pulling alongside genuinely changes the verb. The bigness metric is read against `Player` position in `player.rs`, updated as movement crosses brush boundaries. The wet signal is synthesised entirely in `audio_gen.rs` — a multi-tap delay plus a decaying noise tail, no recorded impulse responses — and applied as a send in `audio.rs`, the same place the truck engine already has its pitch and volume scrubbed live. Per-dimension dryness/tail length tune through the theme palettes in `level.rs`, and it stacks cleanly with the echo-ping and noise-budget ideas, which share the very same probe.

**Build sketch** — Add a `RoomAcoustics` resource holding a smoothed bigness value, updated by a system that casts ~6 rays and averages clamped distances; feed it into a procedural reverb node that `audio.rs` mixes per SFX voice. The honest hard part is two-fold: a probe cheap enough to run every frame on the big level-8 dam without it showing in `QC_FPSLOG`, and synthesising a reverb that actually *sounds* like a room from primitives rather than a real convolution — early-reflection timing and a tail that decays believably are pure DSP tuning.

**Effort** — **M.** Main risk: faking a convincing reverb from synthesised delays alone, without it sounding like a cheap echo box.
